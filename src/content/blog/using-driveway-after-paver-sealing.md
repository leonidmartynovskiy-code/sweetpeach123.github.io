## Make access a booking decision

Tell the contractor before booking if the driveway is your only way into the house. Include home-care visits, mobility needs, school pickups and deliveries that cannot easily move. Establish an alternative before agreeing to a closure.

Walk the proposed route while everything is still accessible. Check the full journey from alternate parking to a usable door, including gates, steps and any space needed for mobility equipment. An open side gate is useful only if the route beyond it is safely available.

- **Parking:** obtain permission for alternate parking and confirm you can enter and leave it without crossing the work area. Keep sidewalks and the street unobstructed.
- **Household vehicles:** include a second car, golf cart or vehicle stored behind the garage. Coordinate each move with the crew before work begins.
- **Essential access:** identify routes needed for care visits and emergency access. Never let a temporary barrier obstruct a needed route.
- **Fallback arrangements:** decide what changes if closure continues beyond the expected reopening point, including collection of a vehicle left elsewhere.

If there is no workable alternate entrance, resolve that with the contractor before scheduling. Do not assume the driveway can be treated in sections while leaving a crossing open.

Ask for instructions that distinguish walking access from vehicle access; dry-to-touch alone authorizes neither. Rain resistance does not establish traffic readiness. Do not decide readiness by touching the coating or taking a trial step. Let the installer apply the selected product's guidance to your site.

Keep the selected product name and its pedestrian and vehicle reopening instructions in the written handoff.

## Can you drive on patio pavers while the driveway is closed?

Sometimes, but "patio pavers" does not identify a vehicle-capable installation. The answer depends on the exact product, its support and edges, the condition of the complete route, and the vehicle using it. A patio that looks like the driveway may have a different assembly beneath the surface.

CMHA's [Application Guide for Interlocking Concrete Pavements](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-010/) distinguishes pedestrian and vehicle applications. It explains that product dimensions, base and soil support, drainage and edge restraints all matter to system performance. Its guidance concerns interlocking concrete pavers; it does not approve an unidentified stone, porcelain or slab patio for vehicle use.

A sealer release answers whether a particular treatment is ready for the stated traffic. It does not certify an adjacent patio as a driveway. Even one planned crossing needs a suitability decision for that route and vehicle. Driving slowly or seeing no damage after a previous crossing does not establish capacity.

If suitability is unknown, keep vehicles off the patio while the installation is assessed. Waiting longer for a coating to cure does not resolve missing product or construction information.

## Gather the patio and vehicle records before approving a crossing

Start with the original estimate, invoices, product sheets, drawings and construction photographs. Ask the original installer what was specified for the patio and whether vehicle use was included. Give the assessing contractor or design professional:

- **Product information:** the manufacturer and product name, unit dimensions and thickness, intended applications and the proposed or existing installation method.
- **Support information:** records of bedding, base or supporting slab, soil preparation and drainage. Identify overlays or raised areas rather than assuming everything is built like the driveway.
- **Edges and condition:** the restraint details and transitions along the full route, plus known settlement, loose units, broken edges, widened joints or water collection.
- **Vehicle information:** the actual vehicle and loaded weight, relevant axle or wheel loads, trip frequency, and where it will turn or stop. Include delivery equipment separately from household cars.

Photographs of the finished surface cannot verify hidden support. Missing records, a change from pedestrian use or signs of movement call for a separate site assessment and appropriate design review. Ask what inspection or investigation is needed before anyone approves the crossing; do not estimate capacity from paver thickness alone.

## Agree on temporary access before a delivery is dispatched

A delivery slot is not confirmation that the driver can cross the patio. Before dispatch, give the supplier the available route and restrictions. Obtain the vehicle and unloading-equipment details, then have the contractor or responsible designer confirm whether the proposed route can accommodate them.

Record the delivery stopping point, unloading location, permitted route and person coordinating arrival. Include any turning area and surface that unloading equipment would cross. Permission for a household car does not establish suitability for a loaded delivery truck, trailer or forklift.

If the route remains unverified or the driveway has not been released, arrange an acceptable alternate drop-off or reschedule. Keep the driver out of closed areas while decisions are made. Do not improvise with boards, mats or a route across the patio after the truck arrives; any temporary protection needs its own agreed purpose and assessment.

This article does not document a completed patio-to-vehicle conversion or a tested temporary crossing. Your access decision needs evidence for your own installation and load.

## Redirect the small trips that cross the driveway

Put the temporary route and delivery point into household plans before the crew arrives, and tell regular visitors what has changed.

- **Pets:** arrange a route and relief area that avoids the treated surface. Tell anyone walking or collecting them which gate to use.
- **Bins:** place them where collection and return can happen without rolling them through the closed area, as agreed with the crew.
- **Packages and home services:** redirect drop-offs to an accessible location. Give carers and scheduled visitors clear arrival instructions.
- **Lawn and irrigation service:** coordinate visits so equipment, hoses and routine watering do not interfere with the closed work area.

Ask the professional handling the treatment what should happen with irrigation and forecast rain. Have them specify any protective measures. Do not cover the coating yourself. Retrieve anything needed from storage reached through the driveway, and check that household members have keys for the alternate door.

## Get a release that names the area and the use

Before the crew leaves, identify who can authorize reopening and how you will receive that decision. A calendar reminder is useful for checking in; it is not permission to remove the barrier. Ask for separate written releases for feet and vehicles if they become eligible at different stages.

Keep the handoff practical:

- Identify the person responsible for confirming reopening and a way to reach them.
- State exactly which area and type of use have been released.
- Record remaining restrictions and what happens if weather changes before the planned return.
- Confirm who moves the barriers and whether another assessment is needed before vehicles return.

Share the release with everyone using the property. Before moving a vehicle back, coordinate its route and parking position with the crew. Keep alternate parking available until that is settled. Heavy stationary planters and furniture need their own installer guidance about when and how to return them.

## If rain or an early crossing interrupts the plan

If you notice a footprint, tire mark or unexpected change after rain, document it from an accessible area and contact the installer. Record what happened, where and when. Share photos without stepping onto the closed surface for a closer view.

Keep others away while you obtain instructions. Do not pressure wash, scrub or recoat the affected area yourself. Ask whether the event changes the release or requires another assessment, then update household and visitor arrangements.
