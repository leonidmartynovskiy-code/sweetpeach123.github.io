If your pavers feel slippery, pause resealing plans and arrange an assessment. Record where and when the concern occurs, identify the surface and previous treatments, and ask the contractor to distinguish observations from possible causes. Keep people off the affected area pending assessment. A photograph, visible texture or finish name cannot establish traction. The useful decision is which investigation or work belongs in the proposal, with resealing left open until the concerns are addressed.

## Record the circumstances without recreating them

Describe an experience that already happened during ordinary use. Do not walk onto wet pavers to demonstrate the problem, slide a shoe across them or invite someone else to compare grip. Keep household members and visitors away from the affected section, and arrange another route while it awaits assessment.

Make a brief record using these prompts:

- **Where:** Name the path, doorway approach or other affected area. Distinguish the center from edges and transitions to adjoining materials.
- **When:** Note whether the surface was wet or dry when the concern arose. Include rain or irrigation only when you observed it.
- **What changed:** Record whether this is a new concern on older pavers, something noticed after recent work, or an issue present since you first used the area.
- **Nearby conditions:** Note irrigation heads, shaded edges and places where water was visible. Their proximity does not prove a cause.

If different household members already reported a concern, preserve their descriptions separately. Note whether they were describing the same place and conditions. Do not turn differing recollections into a request to repeat the experience.

Take photographs from a secure location outside the affected area. An overall view can locate the concern; a closer image is useful only if it can be taken without entering it. Label the location and circumstances. There is no need to wet the surface for a photograph. Images support the conversation but cannot diagnose slipperiness.

## Assemble the material and maintenance history

Find installation descriptions, prior invoices and product records. List any known coating names and application dates, along with cleaning dates, products used and who performed the work. Include a recent treatment even if it was described as routine maintenance. Where records are missing, write unknown instead of guessing from color or shine.

Keep areas separate. The walkway, border and adjoining steps may need individual identification. Ask the assessor to confirm which material each record describes before using it to specify maintenance elsewhere.

## Ask for separate observations, then an explanation

The contractor's assessment should distinguish residue, possible growth, existing coating, material condition and standing water. These are avenues to investigate, not established causes. A useful report says what was observed, what remains uncertain and what further information could change the proposed work.

For example, a visible mark belongs in the record without being automatically labeled algae. Our guide to [green patches on shaded pavers](/blog/green-patches-on-shaded-pavers/) addresses that separate visual concern. Do not let a familiar stain description replace assessment of the slippery-feeling area.

Have the contractor identify the boundary of each observation on a sketch or photograph. If the concern occurs only beside a gate, ask why a proposal includes the entire path. If it spans adjoining materials, ask how the assessment covers each one. This helps compare estimates without assuming that a larger treatment area answers the problem better.

Uneven units or standing-water concerns need separate assessment; sealer is not a structural repair or a correction for drainage.

## Resolve suitability before discussing a new finish

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) warns that overapplication can leave cured sealer slippery. It recommends product trials and following label instructions. Ask the maker about wet and dry slip/skid characteristics and suitability for your actual surface and use.

Keep the manufacturer's response with the proposal. If the response depends on information still missing, record who will obtain it before the treatment decision. Keep any attachments with the response so the assessor can review the same information you received.

Gloss does not automatically mean dangerous, and matte does not automatically mean safe. Our [paver sealer finish test guide](/blog/paver-sealer-finish-test/) helps with appearance approval, which remains separate from evaluating traction. A finish you like is not evidence that the reported concern has been resolved.

If formal traction testing is needed, ask a qualified professional to determine the appropriate assessment and explain its limits. A casual walkthrough or homeowner demonstration is not a substitute. Do not accept a promise of a slipproof finish or assume that another coat is a universal remedy.

## Make each proposed stage a separate decision

The written scope should let you understand what you are authorizing now and what remains conditional. Separate the following items instead of accepting a single promise to fix slippery pavers:

- **Cleaning:** Identify the included area, the condition being addressed and what remains outside the work.
- **Test patch:** Define its purpose, location, review and approval before broader treatment.
- **Possible coating removal:** State whether assessment is still needed, who proposes it and when separate authorization would be requested.
- **Repairs or water-related work:** Identify the responsible specialist and dependencies before scheduling maintenance.
- **Optional resealing:** Keep it as an explicit decision, including the option to defer it while questions remain unresolved.

Ask how findings will be documented between stages. If the contractor proposes changing the work after an initial visit or trial, request the reason and revised boundaries in writing. Agree who can authorize that change. A different product or expanded area should not appear as an unexplained substitution.

Also distinguish the assessment fee, if any, from the proposed work. Ask which stages the quoted amount covers and which require a later proposal. Compare the same included areas and responsibilities across estimates. A price for cleaning alone is not directly comparable with one that also allows for investigation or removal work.

Clarify practical access too: which gates and doors must remain usable, where the work boundary will be, and who communicates restrictions to the household. Resolve an affected route that is the only practical entrance before scheduling work.

## Leave with a record and a defined next step

Keep the observations, material records, manufacturer's response and agreed scope together. The assessment should leave you knowing which questions were answered, which remain open and who handles the next action. An unresolved concern deserves a documented follow-up, rather than an automatic resealing booking. A consultation is not a safety certification.
