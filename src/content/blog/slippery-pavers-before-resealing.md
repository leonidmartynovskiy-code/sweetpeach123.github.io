## Record the circumstances without recreating them

Describe an experience that already happened during ordinary use. Do not walk onto wet pavers to demonstrate the problem, slide a shoe across them or invite someone else to compare grip. Keep household members and visitors away from the affected section, and arrange another route while it awaits assessment.

Make a brief record using these prompts:

- **Where:** Name the path, doorway approach or other affected area. Distinguish the center from edges and transitions to adjoining materials.
- **When:** Note whether the surface was wet or dry when the concern arose. Include rain or irrigation only when you observed it.
- **What changed:** Record whether this is a new concern on older pavers, something noticed after recent work, or an issue present since you first used the area.
- **Nearby conditions:** Note irrigation heads, shaded edges and places where water was visible. Their proximity does not prove a cause.

Keep separate accounts from household members who already reported a concern, noting the place and conditions. Do not ask them to repeat the experience.

Take photographs from a secure location outside the affected area. An overall view can locate the concern; a closer image is useful only if it can be taken without entering it. Label the location and circumstances. There is no need to wet the surface for a photograph. Images support the conversation but cannot diagnose slipperiness.

The contractor's assessment should distinguish residue, possible growth, existing coating, material condition and standing water. These are avenues to investigate, not established causes. ## Resolve suitability before discussing a new finish

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) warns that overapplication can leave cured sealer slippery. It recommends product trials and following label instructions. Ask the maker about wet and dry slip/skid characteristics and suitability for your actual surface and use.

Keep the manufacturer's response and attachments with the proposal. Assign responsibility for any missing information before deciding on treatment.

Gloss does not automatically mean dangerous, and matte does not automatically mean safe. ## Choose the next action from the finding

More than one condition may need attention. Connect each proposed action to a finding and establish the order of work.

### If contamination is confirmed, specify compatible cleaning

For confirmed residue, organic buildup or another removable contaminant, specify cleaning matched to that condition, the pavers and any existing coating. For concrete pavers, the CMHA cleaning guidance calls for stain identification and a small trial before broader treatment.

Have the contractor define the cleaning area, preparation, rinsing and review. Cleaning addresses the contamination; it does not establish traction. If the concern remains, return to assessment instead of automatically adding sealer.

### If the coating is unsuitable, resolve correction or removal first

An unsuitable or excessive coating needs product-specific advice before recoating. Ask the contractor and coating manufacturer whether correction or removal is appropriate, with a compatible method and professional trial before broader work. An unknown coating remains an unresolved compatibility question.

Price removal separately, including its boundaries and any effects on the underlying surface. Do not choose an abrasive treatment, chemical stripper or traction additive from a generic recipe. After correction or removal, reassess the exposed surface and decide separately whether a new treatment belongs in the scope.

### If water or levels are involved, arrange the appropriate repair

Persistent ponding, displaced units or an observed irrigation problem calls for a separate site review. The responsible installer or specialist should establish the cause before proposing changes. Depending on the finding, the work may involve correcting support and resetting units, addressing drainage, or having an irrigation provider correct the water source.

Confirm the repair scope and sequence before scheduling related maintenance. Cleaning a low area does not raise it, and sealing does not correct its drainage. Keep the original traction concern open for follow-up after the site work.

### If the material or finish is unsuitable, evaluate a different surface

If records or assessment raise a material or finish concern, have a qualified professional evaluate the exact product for the actual use. Obtain available product information and any appropriate assessment before deciding on a remedy.

If the surface is found unsuitable, discuss a supported treatment or replacement option with the relevant supplier and installer. Replacement should address the intended use and installation, rather than relying on a non-slip label. No proposed finish guarantees that slipping cannot occur.
