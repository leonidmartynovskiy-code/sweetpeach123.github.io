Before ordering porcelain driveway pavers, verify the exact product, size and finish, the vehicles expected to use it, and the installation assembly documented for that combination. A pedestrian application, material strength claim or thickness alone does not establish driveway suitability. Ask the contractor and supplier to connect the selected product to current manufacturer documents and the proposed work. Resolve gaps before authorizing a nonrefundable order, including who must approve a substitution.

## Identify the product behind the sample

A showroom panel helps you judge appearance, but the order needs a precise identity. Record the manufacturer, collection, product code, dimensions, thickness, color and finish. Have the supplier identify which current application and installation documents cover that exact selection. Save dated copies with the proposal so a later conversation does not depend on a changing web page.

If a document covers a collection, ask where it includes your chosen size and finish. Similar names or matching colors do not establish that different items share the same permitted applications. A brochure photo of a car is also an incomplete answer without the corresponding product and assembly information.

Our [overview of large-format porcelain pavers](/blog/large-format-porcelain-pavers-south-florida/) explores the broader material and design choice. Here, the decision is narrower: whether the item on your order has documentation supporting its proposed driveway use.

## Describe the vehicles and how they use the space

Give the contractor a practical use description: daily parking, reversing from the garage, turning near an edge and any occasional access by service vehicles. Include a trailer or other vehicle you expect to bring onto the property. Let the professionals identify the information needed to evaluate that use instead of translating a material test result into a vehicle allowance yourself.

Distinguish routine use from future possibilities. A household planning to add a vehicle should raise that possibility while comparing proposals. If the plan relies on keeping certain vehicles off the finished surface, make that limitation explicit and decide whether it suits your household. An application label such as light vehicle use needs interpretation for the vehicles and movements actually proposed.

## Match the assembly to the application

The assembly is the whole installation beneath and around the porcelain, including its support, joints, edges and connections to adjoining surfaces. Ask which current manufacturer document covers the selected product on the proposed base. Have the contractor explain how the proposal addresses joints, edge support, drainage and transitions, and identify anything needing further technical clarification.

The [Porcelain Pavers Installation Guide](https://www.belgard.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/Porcelain-Pavers-Installation-Guide.pdf) gives an application-specific example. It places a sand-set assembly over compacted road base in its pedestrian category and a cementitious adhesive assembly over concrete in its light-vehicle category. This illustrates a manufacturer's distinction between assemblies and uses. It neither approves an unspecified product nor establishes a universal restriction for all porcelain pavers.

Keep questions about the existing site separate from the product's application statement. Ask what the contractor has assessed, what remains unknown until removal and how a changed condition would affect the proposed assembly. A plan to retain a base needs its own justification for your project; a past installation elsewhere cannot supply that answer.

## Include the walkway and entry in the scope

Our written scope for a 2026 Palm Beach Gardens project combined a porcelain driveway with walkway and front-entry work. It included removing existing concrete pavers, regrading the retained base and constructing an entry step. That record illustrates how adjoining work expands the scope of a driveway quote. It is not proof of manufacturer approval or technical certification.

For your property, identify where the driveway meets the walkway, garage and entrance. Decide which adjoining surfaces stay and which change. Ask how the selected sizes and layout fit those boundaries, and whether entry work or a change in level belongs in the proposal. Otherwise, two quotes may describe different projects despite naming the same porcelain collection.

## What a reviewable proposal should contain

For a concrete-paver comparison, CMHA's [Guide Specification for the Construction of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-009/) organizes product information, samples and drawings within a project specification. This shows how paperwork connects a material selection to the installation being proposed. Its subject is concrete pavers; it is not porcelain installation guidance, and its construction details should not be transferred to a porcelain proposal.

Use this checklist to compare what each contractor is actually offering:

- **Exact selection:** Product code, size, thickness, finish and color, with the sample identified. Record any alternatives separately so approval of one does not authorize all of them.
- **Intended use:** Expected vehicles, turning and parking areas, plus any stated limitations. Ask who will resolve a mismatch between your use and the application documents.
- **Documented assembly:** Current document titles or revisions and the relevant application. Have the contractor connect those references to the proposed base, joints, edges, drainage and transitions.
- **Work boundaries:** Removal, retained materials, new work and adjacent entry or walkway work. Identify any unresolved site condition and the process for approving a revised scope.
- **Appearance decisions:** Proposed layout, cuts at boundaries and the relationship between driveway and entry finishes. Ask which choices must be settled before the supplier finalizes the order.
- **Responsibilities:** Who confirms product availability, authorizes the order, reviews substitutions and supplies the final care documents. You should know whose answer is still outstanding before committing.

## Read purchase, care and warranty terms together

Ask the supplier when the order becomes nonrefundable and what cancellation, return or replacement terms apply to your selected item. Confirm who owns the order if the contractor purchases on your behalf. Separate acceptance of a sample's appearance from authorization to buy the final quantity and specification.

Read the warranty terms for the selected product and ask about their application to the proposed use and installation. Clarify exclusions, documentation needed for a claim and the distinction between product terms and any separately stated workmanship terms. Do not assume that choosing porcelain creates coverage for the entire driveway.

Get the applicable care instructions before deciding that the finish fits your household. Ask about cleaning products, joint care and any proposed surface treatment. If stock availability prompts a different size, finish or product, reopen the application and assembly review before approving it. A substitution should not proceed merely because the color looks close.

## Decide whether to order, pause or change the selection

You have three practical choices after the review:

- **Proceed:** The product identity, expected use, supporting documents and proposal agree, and the purchase terms are acceptable.
- **Pause:** A document is missing, the proposed use is unclear or the contractor still needs to resolve a site condition that could change the assembly.
- **Reselect:** The available documentation or installation scope does not support the product and use combination you want. Compare a revised selection before committing.
