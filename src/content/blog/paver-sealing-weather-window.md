## Identify the product before choosing the weather window

Ask for the exact product name and its current technical data sheet, often called the TDS. Have the installer identify the requirements relevant to application, moisture and weather protection. A description such as "professional sealer" leaves you unable to check which instructions govern the work.

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) emphasizes manufacturer guidance and describes differences in how coating systems handle moisture. It also warns that unsuitable conditions or excessive application can impair appearance. A generic waiting rule cannot replace instructions for the selected system.

The [PRO Natural 1K product application guidance](https://sealnlock.com/product/pro-natural-1k/) permits application to appropriately prepared pavers that remain damp, provided no standing water remains. It also notes that weather and coating thickness can delay return to use. This documented example contradicts a universal bone-dry requirement; it is not a recommendation or a claim that this company uses the product.

If the proposed product changes, request an updated readiness plan. Do not transfer the previous product's moisture allowance to its replacement.

## Report patterns without trying to certify dryness

Observe from your normal accessible route. You may notice that the shaded side stays damp longer than the exposed courtyard, or that one strip gets wet again after irrigation. Describe the location and timing to the installer. Color alone cannot establish moisture content or readiness, and a photograph cannot certify the substrate.

Useful observations include:

- **Shade:** identify the tree, wall or covered edge beside the area that seems slower to dry.
- **Irrigation:** note the normal watering schedule and any overspray you have already seen reaching the pavers.
- **Roof runoff:** show where water normally lands or crosses the surface during rain.
- **Persistent water:** identify places where water remains after surrounding areas appear clear, without guessing at the cause.

Record what you have seen during ordinary weather and use. Do not run sprinklers, pour water, lift pavers or improvise a moisture test to create evidence. The installer determines what assessment the selected system requires.

Ask the installer to include the shaded and repeatedly wetted areas in that assessment, as well as the most exposed section.

## Separate surface readiness from forecast acceptance

Two questions need answers: does the prepared surface meet the product's requirements now, and can the planned application satisfy its weather requirements? An acceptable substrate does not settle the second question. Equally, an encouraging forecast does not make a wet or incompletely prepared area ready.

Ask when the contractor will reassess the forecast and inspect the work area. Agree on a checkpoint before application, plus a contact method if conditions change after the crew arrives. A forecast reviewed when booking is background information, not final authorization for a later start.

Use three practical outcomes in that conversation:

- **Proceed:** the responsible professional confirms the selected system's conditions and the household arrangements are satisfied.
- **Reassess:** rain, washing or unexpected wetting has changed the conditions, so a new check is needed before a decision.
- **Reschedule:** the installer cannot confirm the requirements can be met, or essential access arrangements are unresolved.

Same-day service advertising does not override those checks. Avoid arranging an event around an assumed completion date simply because washing and sealing sometimes share a visit.

## Organize water sources and household access beforehand

Ask who will manage irrigation around the work area, what changes are needed and who authorizes its return to the normal schedule. If a landscape service controls the system, include that person in the plan. Do not leave the crew and household each assuming the other handled it.

Show the installer the known roof runoff routes and ask how they affect scheduling or protection. Any required gutter, drainage or landscape work needs an identified responsible person. Avoid improvising a diversion that creates another problem elsewhere on the property.

Settle these household decisions before application:

- **Protected route:** identify an entrance that remains usable without crossing the treatment area.
- **Pets:** arrange where they will stay and how their normal outdoor route will change.
- **Furniture:** agree who moves it, where it goes and who will approve its return.
- **Access:** tell the installer about deliveries, care visits or other needs that could conflict with a closure.

These arrangements must still work if the start moves. For the later reopening decision, the [guide to using your driveway after paver sealing](/blog/using-driveway-after-paver-sealing/) explains the separate access planning. Permission to begin sealing is not permission to walk or drive over the finished treatment.

## Put the decision and rescheduling process in writing

The [cleaning and sealing service overview](/services/clean-seal/) distinguishes maintenance options. Use it to clarify whether your proposal includes cleaning, joint re-sanding and sealing, then ask which stages can proceed independently if weather changes. A cleaning appointment should not silently become unconditional approval to seal afterward.

A useful written plan identifies:

- **Decision authority:** the named installer or supervisor responsible for the final go/no-go call, and who covers that role if absent.
- **Governing instructions:** the proposed product and current TDS used for the assessment.
- **Stop conditions:** what site observations or weather changes require a pause or another inspection.
- **Schedule updates:** who contacts you, through which channel, and when the next review will occur.

Distinguish a proposed return appointment from a confirmed application decision. If work is postponed after cleaning, ask what the crew will inspect or prepare again before sealing. Request a written update identifying completed work, remaining work and any proposed scope change. Check your own agreement and clarify any contractual effect of rescheduling with the contractor.

You should be able to reach a specific person rather than decide for yourself whether a darker corner is acceptable. If the office and field crew give different instructions, ask them to resolve the difference before authorizing application.
