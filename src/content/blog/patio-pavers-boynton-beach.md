When a proposed improvement reduces permeable land by more than 800 square feet, the city requires a professionally prepared drainage plan signed by a licensed engineer. That puts engineering on your shopping list alongside materials and labor. A 20-by-40-foot rectangle equals exactly 800 square feet; the published trigger is more than that. ## Measure the lawn you would give up

Stretch a tape from the back wall across the grass. Imagine marking 20 feet out, then measuring 40 along the proposed edge. That rectangle totals 800 square feet. Now pull a chair back from the imaginary dining table. Walk past it as though you were carrying a tray from the kitchen. Comfortable circulation takes room, and that extra strip can push your sketch beyond the original footprint.

The [Engineering Division questions and answers](https://www.boynton-beach.org/321/Engineering) give two descriptions: a reduction in permeable land exceeding 800 square feet, and proposed concrete pavers exceeding that size. Ask the department how it will count your particular project, with the existing surfaces and all additions shown together. This matters when part of the job replaces an old slab and another part covers grass.

## Look beneath the joints

Those narrow seams can be misleading. You look between the individual units and picture rain disappearing into the ground. Underneath, though, a conventional installation has bedding sand over a compacted aggregate base, with more sand in the joints. CMHA describes that construction in [Construction of Interlocking Concrete Pavements, PAV-TEC-002](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-002/). It supports a stable walking surface; it is not a permeable stormwater system.

A purpose-built permeable assembly works differently. Open joints contain stone, and a reservoir base beneath holds water. That difference makes the local classification of ordinary installations understandable. Ask Engineering Division whether the alternative you are considering will receive different treatment before buying it for that purpose. The product label alone gives you no municipal approval.

## What changes in the submission

Spread the certified survey on the kitchen table. With a pencil, trace the proposed terrace beside the house, then mark the poolside extension. Write the total square footage in the margin. The number matters: once the job removes more than 800 square feet of permeable land, a sketch on that sheet needs an engineered drainage plan alongside it.

For minor renovations such as driveways or fences, the municipal FAQ allows the full scope to be shown on a certified survey. The separate requirement above the threshold brings in a licensed engineer and calculations. That is a real addition to the package, with professional services to arrange before submission.

The [Engineering Standards Manual](https://www.boynton-beach.org/760/Engineering-Standards-Manual) provides the Engineering Design Handbook and Construction Standards referenced by the FAQ. The Land Development Regulations point to Chapter 4, Article VIII, Section 3.G. These are the documents your designer uses to put the stormwater solution on paper.

## Watch a storm before choosing the final footprint

After a downpour, stand at the back door. Look along the lawn's edge. A shallow silver ribbon may linger in a grassy depression while the higher ground looks dry. Photograph it. Follow that low strip with your eyes and show it to the designer before covering the grass.

A linear channel at the boundary collects runoff. It also needs somewhere to send it. The adjoining swale, a shallow depression in the ground, has a job of its own. Covering it or changing nearby levels can alter the route rain takes through the yard. Have the engineer show how the new layout keeps that route open or provides another solution.

Next, look at the door threshold and existing steps. Check where the pool coping meets the proposed finish. These fixed edges give the installer levels to measure from. Compacted support, secure borders and carefully matched heights are part of making an outdoor room that stays comfortable to use.

## Budget for the design before booking the gathering

Get the work in writing. The quote should spell out preparation, supporting layers, edges and any specified channels or pipes. A material name and a total price leave too much to guess. If professional services are required, name who will commission them and show how the resulting drawings feed into the construction quote.

## Questions worth settling early

### Does a 20-by-40-foot patio automatically need signed drawings?

That rectangle is exactly 800 square feet. The published trigger is more than 800. It describes the loss of permeable land across the proposal, so an adjoining extension belongs in the same calculation.

### Will a permeable system remove the drainage requirement?

A permeable label alone gives you no exemption. The open joints and reservoir base describe how the system handles rainfall; both belong in the proposed construction details.

### Is HOA permission enough to begin?

Association consent and municipal authorization serve separate purposes. Upload the HOA document and get the necessary approvals in place before the crew starts.

### How should I use the 15-business-day allowance?

Allow it for each review cycle. Leave room elsewhere in the schedule for preparation and responses to comments. Engineering Division's phone number is (561) 742-6000.
