If your pavers look sound but the previous sealer is unknown, do not approve resealing from appearance alone. Recover whatever treatment records are available, identify areas with different histories, and arrange a professional assessment before choosing the next step. Missing records do not automatically justify stripping, and a tidy surface does not establish compatibility with another product. Your initial purchase should be a defined investigation, with a decision about further work after the findings are reviewed, rather than an unconditional commitment to coat everything.

## Recover records without turning recollections into facts

Start with the previous owner and any contractor whose name appears in the home paperwork. Ask for old invoices, the exact product name, label photographs, application dates and the areas treated. Request records of later touchups as well as the original job. A complete invoice for the patio does not establish what happened on the front walkway.

Keep evidence and recollection separate. An invoice naming a product and location is more specific than a seller's memory that the pavers were sealed. A leftover container is a lead to investigate, not proof that its contents were applied throughout the property. Save the original document or photograph alongside your note about who supplied it.

If a contractor can recover a job record, ask whether it describes proposed work or the work actually performed. Mark unanswered questions as unknown. You can bring an incomplete file to an assessment; there is no benefit in filling its gaps with a product that merely sounds familiar.

When records conflict, keep both versions and flag the discrepancy. Give every contractor the same file, including its gaps, so you can compare recommendations against a shared history instead of unknowingly comparing different assumptions.

## Map the property by treatment history

Make a simple sketch or annotate overview photographs. Label the patio, walkway, borders and any other distinct areas. Note replacements, extensions and remembered touchups, even where they now blend in. Tie each record to its location instead of placing every document under a single whole-property assumption.

Separate observations from explanations. Record a shinier strip or a difference in water beading without assigning a chemistry to it. Gloss, droplets and photographs cannot identify the previous sealer. Matching appearances also cannot establish matching application histories.

Take an overview and closer photographs of each uncertain boundary. Give the assessor the map before the visit so apparently uniform areas do not disappear from the discussion. ## Define what the professional assessment must resolve

Ask the assessor to distinguish the paver material, documented treatments and unresolved history. Have the proposed scope identify which areas can be considered together and which need separate review. A confident product recommendation should still have an explanation connecting it to your surface and records.

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) addresses concrete pavers. It calls for identifying stains, trying treatment in a small area, following label directions and preparing the surface before sealing. It does not prescribe porcelain care.

The [PRO Natural 1K product guidance](https://sealnlock.com/product/pro-natural-1k/) provides one example: recoating depends on a small test confirming compatibility and adhesion; failing, peeling or incompatible coatings require removal. This is one product's instruction, not a universal rule, an endorsement or a statement that our company uses it.

For the proposed product, ask which current instructions govern the assessment, who will interpret the results and what information is still needed. Keep those answers with the estimate so a later substitution does not inherit an approval given to something else.

## Give the test a specific, limited job

Agree on the purpose and location of a professional, product-specific compatibility assessment before authorizing it. Ask why the selected location represents the area being considered. If the walkway has an undocumented touchup while the patio has a recorded treatment, ask how that difference will be addressed.

Request a record of the proposed product, preparation evaluated, test location and professional findings. Distinguish your acceptance of the appearance from the assessor's conclusion about suitability. Liking a small treated area is not the same decision as accepting the recommendation for broader work.

A test does not recover the name of every earlier coating, verify an undocumented application date or establish conditions outside the area assessed. It cannot promise future performance across the property. Ask which conclusions apply only to the tested location and which uncertainties remain. The useful result may be a narrower scope or a request for further information.

## Set a decision point before expanding the work

Put the review point in writing. Assessment should lead to a stated recommendation, with an opportunity to approve or decline the next stage.

For each possible next step, ask for:

- **Defer resealing:** An explanation of why work is being deferred and what would prompt another review.
- **Review further:** The unresolved question, the information sought and responsibility for obtaining it.
- **Consider recoating:** A recommendation tied to the named product, assessed areas and recorded findings.
- **Evaluate removal:** The finding that justifies considering removal, its proposed extent and a separate approval step.

An unknown product name alone should not turn the last option into the default. Equally, a satisfactory finding in a limited area should not silently expand the approved boundaries.

## Leave a useful record for the next owner

At handover, request the actual product name and label information, application date, treated areas and any areas left untreated. Add the final scope, test findings, photographs and product-specific care instructions. Keep proposed work distinct from completed work in the folder.

Update the same area map after future touchups or replacements. A new invoice without a location can recreate today's uncertainty. Store a digital copy with the home's maintenance records so the next owner can understand what was applied and where.
