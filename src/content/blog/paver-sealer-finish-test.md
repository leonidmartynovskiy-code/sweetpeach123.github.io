## Separate richer color from a shinier surface

Color enhancement concerns how deep or saturated the pavers look. Sheen concerns reflected light. You might want the muted reds in a driveway to read more clearly while keeping reflections subdued. Describe those preferences separately so that “richer” does not become an accidental request for “glossier.”

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) explains that most sealers darken or enhance paver color, while finishes range from glossy to low sheen or flat. It also advises consulting the manufacturer about suitability for the intended environment and following product directions. That range makes the actual surface a better approval reference than a finish category alone.

Look at the entire color blend, including borders. A change you enjoy on a red unit may make a pale border more prominent than you expected. Decide whether you want to preserve that contrast, bring out subdued colors or keep the surface visually quiet.

## Read beyond the finish name

The [PRO Natural 1K product guidance](https://sealnlock.com/product/pro-natural-1k/) describes limited color enhancement and low sheen. It warns that coatings can change wet traction and requires compatibility and adhesion testing before application over an existing coating. This product-specific example is not a recommendation or a statement that our company uses it.

A pleasing sample does not establish wet traction. Ask the installer to assess the proposed system for its intended use. Do not rank traction by how matte or glossy a finish looks.

Bring old invoices or product information if available. If the previous coating is unknown, record that uncertainty instead of choosing a new product on appearance alone.

## Agree on a representative test area

Ask the installer to propose a small test area and obtain your consent before treating it. Agree on the location, preparation and product first. Also discuss what happens to that patch if you reject the result; do not assume a trial can simply disappear.

Choose an area that includes the colors and surface conditions relevant to your decision. A convenient hidden corner may miss the worn center of a driveway or a contrasting border. Where existing pavers differ substantially, ask whether one test adequately represents the included areas.

The trial should reflect the preparation proposed for the job. Comparing a cleaned and sealed patch with dirty surrounding pavers combines several changes at once. Ask the installer to explain which improvement comes from cleaning and which comes from the finish, using a prepared comparison area where practical.

If your property has an HOA, ask your own association whether the proposed color or finish change needs review. Confirm whether review is needed before authorizing the test itself. A neighbor's past project does not establish your current requirements.

## Concrete paver sealer coverage

Start with the measured treatment area. Mark included driveway sections, borders and connecting walks on a sketch, with exclusions clearly identified. Ask for a breakdown where different areas need different preparation. An estimate based on the entire driveway is not comparable with one covering only its central field.

Use the selected product's actual stated coverage, including its units and conditions. For example, the [PRO Natural 1K coverage and application guidance](https://sealnlock.com/product/pro-natural-1k/) lists approximately 750 to 850 square feet per 5-gallon pail after the specified dilution; absorption and joint width affect actual coverage. That product's figure cannot establish the quantity for another sealer or your property.

Have the installer identify whether the stated rate covers one coat or the complete proposed application. If that basis is unclear, obtain manufacturer clarification before calculating an allowance. Keep packaged product and prepared application volume distinct; neither a container count nor a generic gallons calculator defines the full scope.

Assess the substrate after the agreed preparation, including joint condition and the selected product's moisture requirements. Do not assume an unknown old coating means less material will be needed. Identify any coating removal as separate work, and resolve compatibility before using a trial to support the estimate.

Ask the installer to record the trial's measured area, material used, coats and application method, then compare observed consumption with current product guidance. A trial on one surface condition should not silently set the allowance for differently prepared sections. Document any remaining uncertainty and what further assessment would resolve it.

The material allowance should therefore identify its measured area, product rate and trial basis. Trial observations inform the estimate; they do not override manufacturer limits. If consumption differs from the estimate, pause to establish why and agree on any scope change instead of stretching the material to meet a quoted quantity.

## Review the cured appearance in everyday views

Agree with the installer when the selected product can be assessed and when access is permitted. Once assessment is appropriate, compare the trial from the places where you normally see the surface:

- From the entry or driveway approach, judge the overall color balance and border contrast.
- From a seated patio view, look for reflections that attract more attention than you want.
- In ordinary daylight and shade, decide whether the finish remains acceptable as the light changes.

Photographs can document the agreed area and help explain preferences. Phone screens, exposure settings and reflections make them imperfect color references. Pouring water over pavers is also no substitute for viewing the actual cured system.

Write down a specific response: color acceptable, reflection too noticeable, border too dark, or variation still unresolved. Specific observations give the installer something useful to assess before a larger commitment.

## Put the finish decision and application scope in writing

Your finish approval records the color and sheen you accept. The installer must separately confirm that the proposed rate, coats and application method meet current manufacturer guidance. An attractive sample does not establish correct application, and appearance approval alone should not authorize an unpriced scope change.

Connect the sample to the written quote:

- **Included area and preparation:** Record measured concrete paver areas and exclusions. Record coating history or mark it unknown, and identify cleaning, joint work, repairs and any old-coating removal separately.
- **Product and application:** Name the exact sealer, current manufacturer instructions used, proposed coats and application method. State the coverage basis and material allowance, including any provisional assumptions.
- **Test reference:** Record its location, preparation, product, application details and date of cured appearance review. Keep photographs as supporting documentation.
- **Finish acceptance:** Describe the color and reflection accepted, variation discussed and unresolved concerns. Keep this distinct from the installer's technical assessment.
- **Changes:** Name who approves revised area, preparation, product, coats or price before additional work proceeds. State what triggers a revised quote and, where relevant, another finish trial.

Compare proposals against these same boundaries. Establish whether the trial and additional assessment are included or separately priced. If the product or preparation changes, revisit the approval reference; the original patch no longer represents the same proposed treatment.

## Make the final decision before full treatment

Proceed when appearance is acceptable, suitability questions are resolved and the written scope matches the proposed application. If the color shift or reflection disappoints you, discuss another suitable option before treating the full surface. Your own approved area remains the appearance reference, rather than a photograph from another installation.
