Before listing your home, agree on the areas to clean and the access you need for photography and showings. Cleaning may be enough; sealing is optional, subject to a surface assessment and an acceptable test. ## Define the visitor route and affected areas

Start with the route people will actually use: where they arrive, how they reach the entrance, and which outdoor areas they will visit. A front path, driveway and rear patio may need different work. Name each area separately instead of requesting that the entire property be refreshed.

Photograph the visibly affected sections and include enough surrounding detail to locate them. Describe what you see without diagnosing it: dark marks beside a parking space, a pale patch near the doorway, or missing joint material along an edge. Note recurring concerns and any previous work you can document. These notes give the assessment a starting point.

Gather installation invoices, material names and records of earlier cleaners or sealers. Match each record to its area. If the old product is unknown, say so. A recollection that someone sealed the driveway is useful background, but it does not identify the product.

List intended photography and showing dates, including arrival times and access needed for equipment. Mark which appointments are confirmed and which can move.

## Separate appearance from repair decisions

CMHA's [Operation and Maintenance Guide for Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/) distinguishes aesthetic distress from structural distress. Cleaning does not correct movement; conditions affecting support or stability require their own assessment.

Keep any unresolved concern visible in the proposal. An attractive photograph cannot establish that the surface is sound. Do not conceal defects with a coating, strategic staging or edited listing photos. If a concern needs further investigation, ask who will assess it and what remains outside the cleaning agreement.

This gives you a useful decision point before booking: approve a defined maintenance task, authorize a separately described repair, or pause the affected area while its condition is clarified. Avoid a vague instruction to make everything look finished.

## Ask for a proposal with clear boundaries

A useful proposal lets you identify the work at each location. Request separate entries for:

- **Repairs:** the areas referred for assessment, any agreed repair work, exclusions and responsibility for unresolved conditions.
- **Cleaning:** the included surfaces, specific marks to be addressed, surrounding features to protect and how remaining marks will be reviewed.
- **Joints:** the locations needing attention, the proposed work and whether it is included or awaits further approval.
- **Optional sealing:** a separate decision and scope, with the proposed areas, preparation and access implications identified.

Ask what would trigger a revised proposal. If the contractor finds an unexpected condition, agree that the finding and proposed change will be explained before additional work proceeds. A defined scope can accommodate discoveries without turning a cleaning request into an open-ended treatment order.

## Decide what result is sufficient

CMHA's [Cleaning, Sealing and Joint Sand Stabilization of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-005/) recommends evaluating a small test cleaning and following product labels and warnings. This guidance concerns concrete pavers.

Before full cleaning, discuss how the trial result will be reviewed and who approves proceeding. Ask the contractor to identify any limitation the trial leaves unresolved. An improvement in one selected area does not promise that every mark across the property will disappear.

Define success in terms of the agreed work and a realistic appearance. Do you want accumulated dirt addressed on the approach, particular stains investigated, or a broader maintenance service? These are different requests. Record any marks expected to remain, rather than assuming an instruction to clean includes removing every visible difference.

If the reviewed result meets your objective, consider stopping at the agreed cleaning and joint work. Any sealing decision needs its own assessment and test approval. Do not make perfectly uniform color or a permanent finish an acceptance condition.

If a recommendation for another coat rests on droplets alone, the guide to [water beading on pavers and resealing decisions](/blog/water-beading-pavers-resealing/) provides the separate background. Keep this project's decision tied to the assessed surface and agreed purpose.

Apply that discipline to your own list. Identify a concrete front path separately from porcelain around the pool or a stone border. Ask for material-specific assessment of each included surface; concrete guidance does not provide instructions for every adjacent material.

## Schedule access around photography and showings

For a Palm Beach County home, plan the actual route from parking to the entrance with the contractor. Include the photographer carrying equipment, anyone accompanying visitors and household members who still need to come and go. An alternate door only helps if the full route to it is available and suitable.

Give the contractor the appointment calendar before accepting a work date. Ask which areas will be unavailable, who confirms reopening and how weather will affect the plan. Distinguish pedestrian access from vehicle access, and identify any restrictions on placing equipment or moving objects onto the treated area.

The guide to [using a driveway after paver sealing](/blog/using-driveway-after-paver-sealing/) covers return-to-use planning if sealing becomes part of the scope. For listing preparation, the additional task is coordinating those instructions with booked visits. A photography appointment does not authorize access.

Choose a contact responsible for receiving the contractor's update and passing revised arrival instructions to the people attending. Agree in advance what happens if the planned route remains unavailable: move the appointment or use an alternative the contractor has confirmed. Do not assume a narrow untreated crossing can be left through the work.

Reconfirm access before visitors travel. Keep a fallback appointment available where possible, without treating it as a guaranteed completion date. If weather or findings change the plan, update the visit arrangements along with the work schedule.

## Keep an accurate record of the completed scope

At completion, compare the work record with the approved area list. Keep the actual services performed, products used where applicable, dated photographs, remaining marks, unresolved concerns and contractor instructions together. Distinguish work completed from work considered but declined or deferred.

Use ordinary, unaltered photographs to record the surface. Do not describe the whole property as repaired or sealed when the agreement covered only selected areas. The purpose is an accurate maintenance record of what was done.
