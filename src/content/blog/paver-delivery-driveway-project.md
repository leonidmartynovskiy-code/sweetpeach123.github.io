Before booking the delivery truck, agree with the installer and supplier on the unloading point, where the pallets will be kept, and how your household will walk and park during the work. An open patch of driveway does not prove it can support loaded pallets or handling equipment. Professionals must assess support, access, handling and stacking; homeowners should not lift or move pallets. Settle these decisions before dispatch so the delivery fits both the installation sequence and daily access.

For [driveway pavers in Palm Beach Gardens](/services/driveways/palm-beach-gardens/), delivery planning belongs in the site assessment. Show the installer which entrance you use, where cars need to go, and which surfaces or landscape features you want preserved.

## Mark separate spaces for each stage

A useful delivery plan identifies different activities, even when some can share a location at different times. Walk through these five spaces with the installer:

- **Truck path:** the approach, gate opening, turns and departure route the supplier needs to assess for its actual vehicle.
- **Unloading point:** where the delivery crew can transfer the shipment using its agreed equipment and method.
- **Pallet storage:** where new material can remain while the crew works, with support and access assessed by the professionals.
- **Demolition stockpile:** where removed material awaits reuse or disposal, separately identified from the new order.
- **Clear entry:** the continuous household walking route, protected from storage and active handling operations.

Then compare the plan before removal, during preparation and during installation. A spot available on delivery day may be inside the next area of excavation. Determine whether it is better to change the arrival sequence or have the contractor include planned relocation in the scope.

There is no useful universal pallet count or staging footprint for every driveway. Have the supplier identify the actual shipment and equipment, then let the installer evaluate how those fit the site. A parking space marked on a sketch is not a load-bearing assessment.

## Keep the household route usable

Decide where cars will be parked before the truck arrives and how people will reach the door from there. Tell the installer about mobility needs, school pickups, pet access or scheduled household deliveries. A route that requires stepping around pallets or crossing an active unloading area needs reconsideration.

If your property has an HOA, gate staff or another access arrangement, ask what delivery access, booking or vehicle restrictions apply to your address. Confirm who communicates them to dispatch. Do not nominate a public street or sidewalk for storage without the required approval. Keep lawns, tree areas and drains out of an improvised staging plan.

## Assign a receiver before dispatch

Give the supplier a reachable phone contact and name the authorized receiver. Agree whether that person can approve a changed unloading location, accept a partial shipment or only document what arrives. Being home when the truck comes should not automatically make you responsible for decisions reserved for the installer.

Section 1.05 of CMHA's [Guide Specification for the Construction of Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-009/) calls for original, identified packaging suitable for mechanical handling, coordinated delivery that limits disturbance to building use, and protection from mud and dirt. It is an editable guide specification, not a local law.

Have the receiver compare delivery labels and paperwork with the approved order before workers open bundles. Record the product identification, delivered quantity and any visible damage or mismatch. Photograph labels and keep the delivery record with the order confirmation. The installer and supplier should establish who reports discrepancies and who authorizes any substitution.

If a label does not match, contact them before the affected material enters the work. Do not treat a driver's arrival as approval to change the agreed product. The receiver needs a clear escalation contact, not instructions to make a rushed material decision.

## Separate new stock from material being retained

Protection should follow the actual supplier's instructions for the delivered product. Ask the installer who maintains that protection as work progresses and what happens if packaging is damaged. Do not improvise covers across planting areas or drains to make an unsuitable location usable.

If replacement work retains some existing pavers, CMHA's [Operation and Maintenance Guide for Interlocking Concrete Pavement](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-006/) discusses keeping undamaged removed units and securing them on wooden pallets when they must leave the site. That guidance concerns reusable existing material, a different category from newly delivered stock.

Agree which removed units the installer will evaluate for reuse, where that material belongs and who decides what becomes debris. Record the retained areas before work starts so they are not confused with sections approved for removal. Handling remains the contractor's job.

Where the order adds to an existing surface, resolve [matching new pavers to an existing driveway](/blog/matching-new-pavers-existing-driveway/) before delivery is scheduled. This is a separate appearance decision; delivery paperwork records what was ordered, not whether you will like its relationship to the old surface.

## Agree who communicates access changes

For your installation, establish who tells the household when access arrangements change and when a route is available again. Access conditions and reopening times need to be agreed for your property and the actual work.

## Agree on a failed or incomplete delivery

Before dispatch, decide who responds if the planned location is unavailable, the truck cannot use the agreed approach, or the shipment is short. Supplier dispatch and the installer should agree whether to hold, split or reschedule the delivery and how the installation sequence changes. A delivery problem is not a reason for homeowners to move pallets themselves.

Have any extra handling, additional trip or temporary storage included and priced in the written proposal or an approved written change. Clarify who authorizes it before it happens. Do not assume a return visit is free or that an incomplete shipment can be installed without affecting the agreed work.

If the professionals cannot confirm a workable arrangement, settle the revised plan before sending another truck.

## Decide what stays after installation

An owner reserve and unopened stock intended for return need separate decisions. Agree what reserve you want to retain for future work, where it will be stored and who places it there. Label the product and actual quantity, and keep that record with the final project paperwork.

For surplus stock, verify the supplier's current return terms before treating it as a credit. Confirm eligible packaging and quantities, any return deadline, collection arrangements and charges. Keep unopened stock intended for return distinct from the agreed reserve until the supplier confirms its status. Do not promise yourself a refund based on unused material alone.
