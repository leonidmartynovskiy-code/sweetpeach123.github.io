## Find the options your documents authorize

Collect the declaration, the architectural guidelines authorized by it, and the current application instructions. Check the version of any separate color chart. An old packet from a neighbor can help you find the right document, but verify that it still applies before using it to choose materials.

For an HOA covered by [Florida Statutes 720.3035 (2026)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/720.3035), review authority must be specifically stated or reasonably inferred from the covenants or published guidelines they authorize. The association must apply those standards reasonably and equitably. Where those documents offer options for material, size, design or location, owners may select among the provided options. The association cannot require an issued government building permit before conducting its own review. If it denies a proposal, its written notice must identify both the specific rule or covenant and the particular aspect of the work that does not conform.

Read these provisions alongside your property's documents. They do not guarantee approval or the same outcome under every HOA or condominium arrangement.

Make a short selection sheet with the applicable palette entry beside the product you are considering. If the entry says only a broad color family, have the reviewer clarify how to identify an acceptable product. Save that response with the application. A neighboring deck is useful visual context; its appearance alone cannot tell you what your documents authorize.

## Compare samples with the parts that will stay

At an established golf-community home, begin with the coping, stucco, lanai floor and screen frame. Put physical samples beside those surfaces. View them from inside the house as well as from the pool edge. A deck occupies a large part of that view, and the sample you like on a table may look different beside the existing coping.

Within the documented palette, compare how each choice would meet the existing pool edge.

| Selection approach | What to compare at home | What to identify in the submission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A close match to the coping | Whether the sample looks compatible beside the existing edge | Exact product and color, with a photograph of both surfaces together |
| A contrast border within the authorized palette | Whether the border competes with the pool outline or frames it as intended | Border material, color and location on the layout |
| A different rectangular format | How the proposed pattern meets curves, steps and doorways | Product dimensions and a layout showing the direction of the pattern |

## Submit one version of the deck

Give each proposed material its full manufacturer, product, color and finish designation. Use those same names on the application, sample photographs and contractor's scope. If the form has little room, attach a selection sheet and refer to it by name and date. Avoid describing the same finish as beige in one place and ivory elsewhere.

Show the replacement boundary and label what remains. Mark retained coping as existing and draw a new border in its proposed location. Include visible drains, transitions to the lanai and any steps affected by the work.

Photograph the current deck from a position that includes the pool edge and house. Add a closer view of the selected sample beside the coping. Keep filters off, and identify the physical sample so the photograph can be checked against it.

When several people are helping, choose one person to assemble the final package. Have that person compare the form, layout and material sheet before submission. A last-minute border change should reach every document that shows the border.

## Use the denial to choose the next revision

Read the written response beside the version you submitted. Separate a request for missing information from an objection to the proposed design. If the response says the border conflicts with a palette provision, locate that provision and compare it with the exact border product. Changing the main deck color would leave that objection unresolved.

When the response is vague, request the rule and the disputed feature in writing. Then decide whether to clarify the existing proposal or change it. A product sheet may resolve confusion about a color name. A revised layout may resolve a border location. Send a brief explanation of the change with the replacement documents so the reviewer can follow the revision.

Keep the earlier submission and response for your records, while clearly marking which version you are asking the committee to review now. For a disputed interpretation of the governing documents, have your own attorney review the issue. Hold off on ordering the disputed material while you settle the revision.

## Treat an unavailable product as a new selection

Before committing to an order, confirm that the specified product is available. If a supplier proposes a substitute, obtain its exact designation and a physical sample. Compare the format, finish, color variation and border options with the material in the reviewed package. A similar name is too little information to make that decision.

Send the proposed substitution through the association's applicable review process and get its written response. Update the material sheet, layout where affected, and contractor's scope together. Keep the original product details in the record so everyone can see what changed.

Decide whether you prefer to wait for the selected material or reconsider the finish. Include any effect on the coping or border in that discussion. A substitute that changes the meeting point between surfaces deserves a drawing, even when the main deck color looks close. If cost changes, request a revised written scope and price before accepting the switch.

## Keep the city submission consistent with the selection

Confirm the actual pool deck scope with the city's Building Division. Its [Residential Flatwork, Slab checklist](https://www.pbgfl.gov/DocumentCenter/View/23377/Flatwork-Slab) is a general reference, not a declaration that every replacement follows that classification. It lists an application, structural or engineer plans, and a property survey. Reviews include Building Structural and Development Compliance, with Engineering if applicable. An HOA approval letter is required when the plan reviewer specifies it. Additional requirements may apply.

Use the same replacement boundary and material selections wherever they appear in the city and association submissions. Resolve a change in one package before letting the other describe an outdated design. Obtain the permissions required for your project before work begins.
