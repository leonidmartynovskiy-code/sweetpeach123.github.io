## Identify the product before approving the surface

Ask for the supplier's product name or code, dimensions, thickness, finish, and written outdoor designation. Have the supplier identify whether the face cavities are filled and whether that description applies to the actual product being quoted. A kitchen slab sample is not evidence that an outdoor paver will perform as intended. Keep the intended location, such as an open patio or covered sitting area, on the selection record.

In [Travertine Voids](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/default/assets/file/travertine%20voids%20handout%202019.pdf), the Natural Stone Institute explains that travertine contains natural cavities. Filling the face cannot reach closed interior voids, which can become exposed later. Filled does not mean solid through the thickness. Its hotel interior example explains this mechanism, not outdoor suitability.

For a filled product, request available filler identification and technical data alongside the stone information. Ask who can confirm the proposed combination for the intended exposure and maintenance products. If the supplier cannot identify the filler, record that unresolved point before committing. A verbal description such as outdoor stone leaves too much unspecified when several products are under discussion.

## Compare a sample range you can live with

Ask to see several representative pieces of each candidate, including examples that show the range the supplier expects to deliver. Look at the faces closely, then step back to the distance from which you will usually see the patio. Decide whether you like the appearance across the group, rather than choosing the single piece with the most appealing color.

View samples in the light of the proposed area when practical. Note which visible patches, pores, color changes, or edge details you find distracting. This is an appearance review; touching a sample or looking at its texture is not a substitute for relevant slip-performance information. Ask the supplier what documentation applies to the exact finish and intended use.

Keep photos and product identifiers together. Ask whether the samples represent the proposed supply and how deliveries will be reviewed against the approved range. Agree who will raise a mismatch before installation starts. If your preference depends on every piece resembling the least marked sample, request a wider selection before approving the order.

Make your preference specific. If visible open pores are what you like, ask which proposed steps could change that appearance. If you prefer the appearance of the filled sample, ask whether any additional face work is proposed and request an example of the intended result. Neither preference settles the technical selection; it tells the supplier what appearance you are approving.

## Keep filling, jointing, and sealing separate

Filling places material into cavities in the stone. Jointing addresses the spaces between individual pavers. A penetrating sealer is not a substitute for filling holes. An estimate that mentions sealing has not, by that wording alone, explained how visible cavities or joints will be handled.

The Natural Stone Institute's [Care & Cleaning of Natural Stone](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/) starts with identifying the stone and its composition. It explains that calcareous stone is sensitive to acids and that impregnating sealers improve stain resistance without making stone stain proof.

Have the contractor describe the proposed work under separate headings: the supplied face condition, any additional cavity treatment, joint treatment, and any sealing. Ask for product information and confirmation of the proposed materials together. Do not assume a named joint material is also the intended face filler, or that every treatment is included in one installation line.

## Put each area and treatment in the estimate

Consider a project with a covered sitting area and an open walkway. Even if you want one continuous appearance, ask the contractor to identify the product and proposed treatment in each zone. Discuss transitions to existing surfaces and whether adjoining areas are included. These are project questions to resolve through a site review, without assuming a universal installation detail.

A verified Boynton Beach written project scope assigns pavers salvaged from a pool deck to a front walkway, with separate pool-deck work. Its useful lesson is to state each zone and its material allocation in writing. It does not establish a travertine installation, filler performance, or work in West Palm Beach.

Compare proposals against the same selection record. Look for the exact stone, approved sample range, face condition, included treatments, and responsibility for resolving substitutions. Ask how a proposed change would be documented and priced before approval. A total alone cannot tell you whether two contractors have allowed for the same material or the same finishing work.

Also separate the initial work from future service. Ask whether a later assessment is included, what any written warranty covers, and which maintenance or repair decisions would require another proposal. Keep the actual terms with the product records. Do not substitute an informal assurance about natural stone for a written description of the work being offered.

## Agree on follow-up before installation

At handover, retain the approved selection record, supplied product information, care guidance, and dated overview photographs. Ask whom to contact about a surface change and what information they need. That gives a future inspector a starting point and avoids depending on memory about which stone or treatment was used.

A newly visible hole does not, by itself, establish defective material. Arrange an assessment by the installer or an appropriate stone professional. Send a close photograph, a wider view locating the spot, when you first noticed it, and the maintenance history. Ask for an explanation of the finding and a written recommendation before authorizing work.

Discuss whether the recommendation addresses an individual location or calls for a broader review, how any proposed treatment will be selected, and what appearance to expect afterward. Ask which decisions remain uncertain until inspection. Avoid choosing a repair material from a photograph or treating a generic repair suggestion as confirmation for your particular pavers.
