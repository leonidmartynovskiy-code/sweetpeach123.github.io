Start with the project: a driveway carries vehicles, a patio supports outdoor living, and a pool deck needs particular attention to wet conditions and barefoot comfort. Then compare identifiable products, their surface samples and the complete installation proposed for your property.

## Define the use before choosing a material

Write down what the surface must accommodate. Include everyday activity and occasional demands that could change the specification.

- **Driveway:** Identify the vehicles that will use it, including occasional service vehicles. The product and installation need to suit that traffic. Our [driveway pavers installation service](/services/driveways/) addresses this different scope; a pool-deck selection should not automatically carry over to the driveway.
- **Patio:** Mark dining areas, movable furniture and any planned outdoor kitchen. Tell the contractor about heavy fixed features so their support can be addressed separately.
- **Pool deck:** Identify barefoot routes, splash areas, shaded sections, the pool edge and access to equipment. Note whether doors, coping and drains will remain.

For a South Florida property, record actual sun exposure and recurring wet areas. If the site receives salt spray or uses a saltwater pool, include that in the product discussion. These are conditions to verify for your home, not assumptions about every regional project.

## Build a shortlist across the material families

Use appearance to narrow the field, then ask a different question of each candidate. A material family is a starting point; the quoted product still needs its own checks.

### Concrete pavers

Compare the proposed shape, color blend and face texture together. View a group of pieces beside the house rather than choosing from a small color chip. Ask which sizes and installation methods suit the intended traffic, and what the manufacturer recommends for cleaning and any treatment. Compare concrete on the complete installed scope without assuming it will always be the least expensive option.

### Travertine

Shortlist travertine when you like the natural variation visible in the actual selection. Ask to see the expected range, including surface openings and filled areas, and identify the finish being quoted. Decide whether you accept those characteristics before discussing layout. Its name alone cannot settle comfort in direct sun or suitability for a wet pool surround.

### Porcelain

Start with an identifiable outdoor porcelain product and finish. Look at its design across several pieces, including any printed pattern repetition, and ask how cuts, joints and pool-edge details will be handled. Keep the product's installation and care instructions with the quote. A contemporary appearance does not establish vehicle suitability or eliminate cleaning of the finished surface.

If these last two materials make your shortlist, our detailed [travertine versus porcelain pavers comparison](/blog/travertine-vs-porcelain-pavers-south-florida/) covers sample approval, sun exposure and care in greater depth.

### Clay brick pavers

Consider clay when its brick character suits the house and landscape. Review the actual color range, edges and proposed pattern alongside the surrounding finishes. Ask for the intended-use guidance for the selected product. Do not turn a preference for its appearance into an assumption that every clay product suits vehicle traffic or will remain visually unchanged.

### Shellstone and coquina

Do not accept these names as interchangeable geological labels or as a complete specification. Ask the supplier to identify the actual stone, its source, composition and intended exterior use. Review the shell texture and surface variation across samples. A coastal appearance does not establish local origin, heat performance or suitability beside a pool.

### Marble

Consider marble for the veining and finish you actually want, then check the proposed exterior application and care requirements. A honed finish alone does not prove suitability when wet. Review the selection beside the coping and house finishes, and ask how it should be maintained. For a driveway, request documentation for vehicle use before keeping the product on your list.

## Check the exact product and surface sample

Ask for the supplier, product name, finish and quoted dimensions, plus written guidance for the intended application and installation method. Resolve any missing information before approving the material.

For example, the [Quarziti 2.0 product overview](https://www.belgard.com/product/quarziti-2-0/) lists patios, pool decks, rooftops and walkways. That list does not provide universal driveway approval. For vehicle use, obtain documentation addressing the exact product and proposed installation. Use this example to check the application information for whichever product appears in your quote.

Review samples in the planned location, including sun and shade at relevant times. Compare dry and wetted appearance on a stable display. Ask for the applicable wet-use information and its limitations. Sample handling helps you judge appearance and texture; it does not certify slip resistance or guarantee the finished deck's temperature.

## Resolve transitions and drainage before ordering

Walk the proposed area with the contractor. Identify every meeting point: pool coping, door thresholds, steps, drains, adjacent paths and retained surfaces. Ask how the finished heights and edges will work together. A preferred piece size may need reconsideration once borders and cuts are laid out.

For a pool-deck consultation in Palm Beach Gardens, bring photos taken after rain if water collects on the existing surface. Show where it remains and where it appears to travel. The assessment should address water movement across the proposed deck and the condition of retained features. Changing the surface material alone is not a drainage plan.

## Compare the quoted system and its care

Have each estimate cover the same area and explain these items separately:

- **Preparation:** Removal, disposal, retained surfaces and how unexpected conditions will be handled.
- **Installation:** Base or substrate, setting method, joints and edge support for the selected product and use.
- **Connections:** Coping, steps, drains, finished heights, borders and cuts, with exclusions identified.
- **Handover:** Cleanup, any specified treatment and written care instructions for the surface and joints.

This makes a price difference easier to understand. Check whether it buys a different finish or includes work another estimate leaves unresolved.

For stone, the Natural Stone Institute's [Care & Cleaning of Natural Stone](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/) connects cleaning choices to composition and explains the sensitivity of calcareous stone to acids. It also says sealing is not necessary for every stone and does not make stone stainproof. Ask the supplier for guidance specific to your selection, including whether treatment is appropriate. For concrete, clay and porcelain, request the corresponding product instructions instead of assuming one care routine fits all surfaces.

Keep candidates whose intended use is documented, whose samples you accept and whose complete scope and care you understand. If a selection leaves an important question unanswered, resolve it before ordering.
