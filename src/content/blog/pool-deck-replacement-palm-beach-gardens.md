A pool deck remodel in Palm Beach Gardens starts with deciding what can remain. Consider localized repair for a limited problem whose cause has been assessed, lifting and replacing pavers when broader work is needed, and an overlay only when an existing concrete slab suits the proposed system. None of these routes can be approved from a photograph. Unknown support, movement or unresolved drainage calls for investigation before installation is authorized.

For [pool deck pavers installation in Palm Beach Gardens](/services/pool-decks/palm-beach-gardens/), compare what each proposal removes, retains, and changes. The finish matters, but it comes after deciding how the replacement will meet your home and pool.

## Identify the existing layers

Ask the estimator to name the existing layers and explain how they were identified. Old invoices or construction photographs can help. If nobody knows what lies beneath the visible surface, record that uncertainty instead of letting the proposal quietly assume a suitable base.

Be precise about overlay: here it means new pavers installed over an existing concrete slab. A decorative coating or a new surface over loose old pavers is a different proposal. Have the contractor describe the proposed assembly in writing before comparing estimates.

## Choose a route after assessment

### Localized repair

Start here if the problem appears limited. Ask whether an on-site assessment supports repairing that area while retaining the surrounding deck. Define the repair boundary and whether replacement pavers are available. If the cause is unknown, a depression keeps returning or movement extends beyond the proposed patch, pause for further assessment before agreeing to another spot repair.

### Lift and replace

Compare this route when the assessed correction needs access beneath a wider area, or your desired finish requires broader replacement. Lifting pavers exposes the support; it does not establish that every underlying layer needs replacement or can remain. Ask what findings will determine whether the base is retained, corrected or replaced.

### Overlay a concrete slab

Consider this route only after assessing the slab, proposed finished heights and drainage for the chosen assembly. Stop if any remains unknown. Rule out the proposed overlay design if it cannot accommodate doors, coping, drains or gate operation, unless those conflicts are resolved in the scope. Unexplained movement or water collection needs assessment before the slab is covered.

## Measure the junctions

Walk the route from the sliding door to the pool and out through the gate. Ask the estimator to record existing and proposed finished heights at those junctions, including drain rims and any adjoining lanai or walkway. A general statement that everything will be made level is too vague to explain the intended result.

Open the gate during the visit. Point out door tracks, access covers, and places where water has collected. Ask how the proposed work affects each feature, including anything that must be adjusted or replaced. Photograph these conditions before removal so they remain part of the conversation after the old deck is gone.

CMHA's [Application Guide for Interlocking Concrete Pavements](https://www.cmha.org/resource/pav-tec-010/) describes pool surrounds with surface and base drainage toward stormwater drains, drainage at bedding level, and a movement joint at the coping. Its overlay discussion also addresses bedding drainage over concrete. This is guidance for concrete pavers, not an engineering specification for travertine or approval of your existing slab.

Ask for an on-site measurement of the deck and critical junctions, followed by a written scope identifying the proposed route, retained features, and questions still requiring assessment.

## Decide what happens to coping

Coping is the finished edge around the pool. Decide whether keeping it is a preference or a firm boundary of the project. Ask what inspection is needed before that decision becomes part of the contract. If replacement is proposed, have the pool-edge work identified separately from the main deck area so its responsibilities are clear.

Tell the estimator about suspected leaks, past pool repairs, loose coping, or planned work on the pool interior. Ask which questions need a pool specialist and who coordinates that work. A deck proposal should identify whether pool equipment, plumbing, interior finishes, and related repairs are included or excluded.

If you are considering travertine, our [natural stone installation service](/services/natural-stone/palm-beach-gardens/) is relevant to the material and edge-detail discussion. Review a physical sample beside the coping that will remain. Record the selected finish and pattern; do not treat the word travertine as a complete installation specification.

The [travertine pool deck planning guide](/portfolio/hollywood-residence/) lists the material, coping and access questions to discuss before accepting a replacement scope. It is a planning guide, not a completed project record. Keep the desired look and the assessed construction approach as separate decisions until the proposal connects them.

## Compare what the price includes

Give each bidder the same marked work boundary and identify access constraints. Compare removal, disposal, protection, preparation, installation and cleanup. Mark each item as included, separately priced, pending inspection or excluded. Then check the differences between routes:

- **Repair:** Identify the affected area, pavers to reuse or replace, corrections beneath it and the transition into the retained deck. Discuss any visible mismatch before accepting the finish.
- **Lift and replace:** Identify the layers to remove and whether base correction is included or awaits inspection. Separate coping, drain and adjoining-surface adjustments from the main deck work.
- **Slab overlay:** Identify slab preparation, the proposed assembly, bedding drainage and height adjustments. Avoid comparing its surface-material price with a replacement price that includes removal and corrections.

## Authorize hidden work in stages

Agree on these steps before removal starts:

- **Before exposure:** Define any investigation or initial removal separately, including its price, limits and responsibility for protecting the pool and securing the work area during a pause.
- **After exposure:** Request photographs and written findings about the actual substrate. Have the contractor explain what can remain, what needs correction and whether specialist assessment is needed. Photographs document findings; they do not replace that assessment.
- **Before covering:** Approve the resulting route, correction scope, price and any schedule change in writing before extra work or installation proceeds. If condition, finished heights or drainage remain unresolved, keep that authorization pending. Initial removal approval should not become blanket approval for hidden repairs.

## Confirm the local review

The City of Palm Beach Gardens' [Residential - Flatwork, Slab](https://www.pbgfl.gov/DocumentCenter/View/23377/Flatwork-Slab) checklist lists a property survey and structural/engineer plans. Use those documents to make the proposed layout and work boundary clear for review. It names Building Structural and Development Compliance review, with Engineering when applicable. An HOA approval letter is required when specified by the plan reviewer. The checklist is a general reference, and additional requirements may apply. Confirm the exact repair, replacement or overlay scope with the Building Division; this document alone does not determine whether your remodel requires a permit or qualifies for an exemption.
